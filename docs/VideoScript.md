# JustType Keyboard — Introductory Video Script

**Format:** Screen capture with voiceover
**Target length:** 5 minutes
**Audience:** AAC professionals and potential beta testers

---

## OPENING (0:00–0:30)

**[Screen: JustType keyboard visible in a messaging app, idle]**

> For people with significant motor disabilities, typing on a smartphone or tablet can be extremely difficult — or simply impossible. A standard on-screen keyboard presents over thirty small targets. For someone who controls a pointer through head movement, a joystick, or switch scanning, hitting the right key is slow, exhausting, and frustrating.
>
> JustType is a different kind of keyboard. It has just eight keys — and it can be operated through head tracking, a joystick, switch scanning, or direct touch.

---

## THE EIGHT-KEY CONCEPT (0:30–1:30)

**[Screen: Close-up of the JustType keyboard showing the 3×3 grid with center label]**

> Here's the JustType keyboard. Eight large keys arranged around a center information area. Each key is labeled with a group of letters — for example, this key shows I, S, K, and W.

**[Screen: Highlight each key briefly as it's mentioned]**

> Instead of hunting for individual letters on a cramped keyboard, the user just aims for one of eight large, widely-spaced targets. The system figures out which word was intended.

**[Screen: Begin typing "hello" — press keys in sequence]**

> Let's type the word "hello." I press the key containing H… the key containing E… L… L… and O.

**[Screen: Show "hello" appearing as the top prediction]**

> After just the second key press, "hello" appears as the top prediction. JustType's vocabulary of over 50,000 words, ranked by frequency, means the most common word matching a key sequence almost always appears first.

**[Screen: Show the SELECT key and cycle through a few alternatives]**

> If I wanted a different word — say the sequence happened to match another word — I'd press SELECT to cycle through alternatives. But in practice, the top prediction is correct the vast majority of the time.

## TYPING IN ACTION (1:30–2:30)

**[Screen: Clear the text field. Type a short sentence: "Meet me at the park"]**

> Let's type a sentence: "Meet me at the park."
>
> Watch what happens between words. I never press a spacebar — JustType inserts spaces automatically. When I start typing the next word, the previous word is finalized and a space appears.

**[Screen: The sentence builds up word by word with auto-spaces appearing]**

> Notice how quickly the words appear. Each word takes just a few key presses — typically one per letter — and there's no need to aim for tiny individual letter keys.

**[Screen: Type a period from the Symbols page]**

> For punctuation, I press the Symbols key to access periods, commas, question marks, and more. The punctuation is placed correctly — no extra space before the period.

**[Screen: Start typing the next sentence — show auto-capitalization]**

> And when I start the next sentence, JustType automatically capitalizes the first letter.
> I can type virtually anything using just eight keys. 
> (Type "Around 3:00?")

## INPUT METHODS (2:30–3:30)

**[Screen: Show the Settings > Input Methods screen]**

> What makes JustType especially powerful for accessibility is the range of input methods it supports. The same eight-key layout works with all of them.

**[Screen: Show head tracking in action — pointer moving, keys highlighting and activating]**

> **Head tracking** uses the device's front camera — through our companion app HeadBoard — to translate head movements into key presses. Nod your head in the general direction of a key to highlight it, as as soon as it flashes green, return toward the center and gesture toward the next key. Every aspect of sensitivity and timing is adjustable.

**[Screen: Brief clip of joystick input, or show the joystick setup screen]**

> A **physical joystick** or gamepad works similarly — tilt in the direction of the key you want.

**[Screen: Show scanning mode — highlight advancing through keys]**

> **Switch scanning** is designed for users with more limitations on movement. A single switch — or even a touch anywhere on the screen — is all you need to select a key with single switch scanning. Scanning is faster than you'd expect when you only need to select from among eight keys.  Press the switch when the right key is highlighted. Scan speed, repeat behavior, and many other parameters are all adjustable.

**[Screen: Show two-switch mode briefly, typing "Easy!"]**

> **JustType's Two-Switch mode** is unique.  Two switches — conventionally colored red and green — can be used to select any key with exactly three switch presses, and without  any timing requirements on when a switch is pressed. At the start, half the keys are highlighted red and the other half green.  All that is needed is to look at the key you want and press the switch that matches the highlighting color on the key.  Doing that three times — each time, watching what color the key changes to — activates the key.  With only a little practice, a user can learn the pattern for each key (e.g. red-green-red) and become faster and faster.  Switches can be external hardware or left/right halves of the touch screen if no hardware is available.

## SMART FEATURES (3:30–4:15)

**[Screen: Show typing "Mr. Jones" — demonstrate abbreviation period handling]**

> JustType handles the details that matter. Abbreviations like "Mr." and "Dr." include the period, but the keyboard knows it's not the end of a sentence — it correctly spaces the next word and doesn't auto-capitalize.

**[Screen: Show typing a number like "9.5" in a sentence]**

> Decimal numbers are handled the same way — the period in "9.5" doesn't trigger a new sentence.

**[Screen: Show speech output — type a sentence and have it spoken]**

> Built-in speech output can speak each word as it's typed, or wait and speak complete sentences. For users who rely on text-to-speech for communication, the keyboard becomes a voice.

**[Screen: Show typing in a URL bar — demonstrate auto-space suppression]**

> The keyboard also adapts to context. In a URL bar or email field, it automatically disables auto-spacing so web addresses and email addresses can be typed as continuous strings.

---

## WHO IS JUSTTYPE FOR? (4:15–4:50)

**[Screen: Return to the keyboard in a messaging app, ready to type]**

> JustType is designed for people with complex communication needs — people with conditions like ALS, cerebral palsy, spinal cord injury, or muscular dystrophy — who need to type on a mobile device but cannot use a conventional keyboard.
>
> It's also built for the professionals who support them. JustType's extensive configuration options mean that a therapist or assistive technology specialist can tailor the keyboard to a specific individual's abilities — adjusting sensitivity, timing, scan speed, feedback, and input method to find the combination that works best.

### Style Notes

- Keep the voiceover conversational and clear — avoid jargon where possible.
- Let the screen captures do the heavy lifting. Pause the narration during key typing sequences so viewers can watch the interaction.
- Use a clean, uncluttered messaging app for demos (large font, high contrast).
- Consider adding subtle annotations (arrows, highlights) to draw attention to specific UI elements during the explanation, but don't overdo it.
